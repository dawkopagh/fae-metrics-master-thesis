\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis set out to evaluate Feature Attribution Explanation methods under
multiple, potentially conflicting effectiveness criteria, treating the
evaluation pipeline---rather than any new explanation method or predictive
model---as the unit of contribution. The motivating problem is well documented:
the proliferation of XAI evaluation metrics has not produced consensus, because
the metrics disagree, partially duplicate one another, and are themselves of
uncertain reliability. The response developed here is a single, reproducible
pipeline that prunes redundant metrics, discounts unreliable ones, and
aggregates the survivors into an effectiveness index with explicit, auditable
configuration. This chapter summarises the outcome against each of the four
contributions and outlines the directions that the work opens.

The empirical yield of the full 600-image run is, in candour, more cautionary
than triumphant: the cleanest contribution is a redundancy result, while the two
literature-derived enhancements the thesis set out to test deliver a negligible
or model-dependent benefit. These negative and conditional findings are not a
shortfall but the substance of the contribution---each warns against an
assumption that practitioners and the prior literature make too readily.

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

The four contributions map onto what the thesis set out to do as follows. Each
paragraph states the methodological outcome---what was developed, proposed, or
tested---and then the quantitative verdict the full run returns.

\paragraph{Contribution~1 --- Non-redundancy analysis of FAE evaluation
metrics.}
The thesis develops a correlation-based redundancy analysis across the six
Quantus categories (Section~\ref{sec:exp-redundancy}), with a documented
pruning rule, and derives a reduced, non-redundant metric set $M^{*}$. The
methodological outcome is a transferable procedure for diagnosing and removing
metric redundancy before any comparison is made, directly addressing the
``many correlated metrics overstate evidence'' problem identified by
\citet{tomsett2020sanity} and \citet{kadir2024evaluating}.
\emph{Verdict.} This is the strongest and cleanest result of the thesis. On the
full 600 images, two metric pairs proved near-perfectly redundant on both
architectures---Complexity (complexity~$\leftrightarrow$~sparseness,
$|\rho| = 0.999$ on ResNet-18 / $0.993$ on SqueezeNet) and Robustness
(avg-~$\leftrightarrow$~max-sensitivity, $|\rho| = 0.93$ / $0.98$)---while the
Faithfulness and Localization metrics carried independent signal
($|\rho| < 0.25$). Greedy pruning therefore reduced the twelve metrics to an
\emph{identical} seven-metric set $M^{*}$ on \emph{both} models
(faithfulness\_correlation, max\_sensitivity, pixel\_flipping, pointing\_game,
random\_logit, relevance\_mass\_accuracy, sparseness). That two different
architectures yield the same $M^{*}$ shows the redundancy is a property of the
metric battery rather than of the classifier, so an evaluator quoting ``twelve
metrics'' is in effect quoting roughly seven---and can prune the rest with no
loss of discriminative signal.

\paragraph{Contribution~2 --- Multi-criteria aggregation framework, and a test
of reliability-discounted weighting.}
The thesis implements a framework that integrates MetaQuantus meta-validation
\citep{hedstrom2024metaquantus} with the Autoweighted aggregation of
NormEnsembleXAI \citep{hryniewska2024normensemblexai}, and uses it to
\emph{test}---rather than merely assert---whether a MetaQuantus-discounted
weighting operator (Equation~\eqref{eq:mq-discount}), which attenuates each
metric's weight by its measured reliability, changes the FAE ranking. Two
findings result, and honesty requires stating both as they are. First, a
positive but \emph{conditional} one: how much the ranking changes with the
weighting scheme is model-dependent. On ResNet-18 the uniform and Autoweighted
method-rankings agree only moderately (Spearman $\rho = 0.75$; Occlusion stays
first under both, but Grad-CAM drops from second to fourth while Saliency and
Integrated Gradients move up), whereas on SqueezeNet they
agree almost perfectly ($\rho = 0.96$, Grad-CAM first under both). This is
evidence that aggregation \emph{can} materially reorder an FAE ranking---a
warning against
single-number leaderboards---but not that it always does; the most plausible
mechanism is that the inverse-variance Autoweighted weights redistribute weight
differently according to each model's per-metric score-variance structure.
Second, and decisively, a \emph{null} result: the proposed MQ-discount is
empirically inconsequential relative to plain Autoweighted. It correlates at
$\rho = 1.00$ with the Autoweighted
ordering on both models, moves $0/7$ method positions, and shifts effectiveness
by $|\Delta|\approx0.02$ on average; it changes no conclusion. The discount also
rests on partial meta-validation---only five of the seven $M^{*}$ metrics carry
a reliability score---so it could not have been promoted as an improvement even
had it moved the ranking. The contribution is therefore the measured negative
finding itself: a reliability-discounted aggregation, tested on real data, does
not change the verdict that Autoweighted already reaches.

\paragraph{Contribution~3 --- Empirical characterisation of metric ensembling.}
The thesis uses the framework to characterise where aggregation of metric
scores produces rankings that diverge from single-metric rankings and where it
does not, and to compare individual against NormEnsembleXAI-ensembled
attributions (Sections~\ref{sec:exp-ranking}--\ref{sec:exp-ensemble}). The
methodological outcome is an experimental protocol for isolating the effect of
aggregation, and of attribution ensembling, on method ordering.
\emph{Verdict.} Both halves of this analysis return cautionary results. On the
metric-aggregation side, a single-metric ranking (Faithfulness Correlation
alone) is not a safe proxy for the multi-criteria index---the two correlate at
only $\rho = 0.46$ on ResNet-18 and $0.89$ on SqueezeNet---confirming that the
metric family chosen, and how it is weighted, can change which method wins. On
the attribution-ensembling side, the result is \emph{model-dependent and partly
negative}. Over all seven $M^{*}$ metrics and the full 600 images (Wilcoxon
$n = 600$, $p < 10^{-3}$ throughout), the NormEnsembleXAI ensemble beats the
\emph{mean} individual method on SqueezeNet ($0.075$ vs.\ $-0.069$) but
\emph{loses} to it on ResNet-18 ($-0.012$ vs.\ $0.019$), and it never beats the
\emph{best} individual method on either model ($\Delta = -0.305$ and $-0.135$).
Averaging attributions helps a weak, dispersed method set but dilutes a strong
gradient signal once Robustness is counted; ensembling is thus a reasonable
default on the weaker architecture, not a general improvement, and never a
substitute for identifying the per-model best method.

\paragraph{Contribution~4 --- Empirical validation on ISIC~2017.}
The thesis validates the framework on expert-annotated dermoscopic data, using
the segmentation masks to make the Localization category measurable, and
applies Friedman, Nemenyi, and Wilcoxon testing to assess the significance of
method and ensembling differences (Section~\ref{sec:exp-stats}). The
methodological outcome is an end-to-end, statistically grounded instantiation of
the framework on a real, mask-annotated medical task.
\emph{Verdict.} The differences the framework reports are statistically real,
not sampling noise. The Friedman omnibus test over the seven methods and 600
image-blocks rejects equal mean ranks for every scheme and model at
$p < 10^{-3}$ (ResNet-18 $\chi^2 = 760$ Autoweighted / $879$ MQ-discount;
SqueezeNet $\chi^2 = 1430$ / $1412$), with a Nemenyi critical difference of
$\mathrm{CD} = 0.368$. The paired Wilcoxon tests confirm all four ensemble
comparisons are significant at $p < 10^{-3}$. The statistics therefore
establish that the model-dependent ensemble result and the weighting-driven
ranking divergence are genuine effects of the framework's configuration rather
than artefacts of the particular draw of images---which is precisely what makes
the cautionary findings above defensible.

Taken together, the four contributions answer the thesis objective not with a
single ``best'' FAE method---which would be dataset- and configuration-specific
and of limited durability---but with a principled, reproducible apparatus for
reaching such a verdict, and with an honest empirical characterisation of where
that apparatus changes a conclusion (substantial metric redundancy; a
model-dependent ranking divergence under different weightings) and where it does
not (the reliability discount, which proved inconsequential, and ensembling,
which helps only the weaker model and never beats the best method). The yield is
deliberately cautionary: its main practical lesson is to distrust single-number
FAE leaderboards and to test, rather than assume, that any aggregation
refinement earns its place.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

The framework was deliberately bounded in scope (Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}),
and each boundary suggests a concrete extension. The directions below are
ordered roughly from those that consolidate the present results to those that
broaden the framework's reach.

\paragraph{Scaling the statistical analysis.}
The redundancy and reliability estimates rest on a finite sample from a single
challenge distribution. Although both analyses were computed on the full
600-image test set, the immediate next step is to repeat them on repeated
subsamples, reporting confidence intervals on the correlation and reliability
estimates and a stability analysis of the membership of $M^{*}$ and of the
metric weights. This would move the metric structure from a property of one
draw of images to a property of the domain. Completing the meta-validation
itself belongs to the same consolidation: the Adversarial-Reactivity leg did
not complete for \emph{max\_sensitivity} and \emph{random\_logit}, so their
reliability currently rests on the Noise-Resilience leg alone rather than the
full combined score. A related refinement concerns the pruning policy: on this
dataset the greedy cross-category rule of Decision~D4 and its strictly
within-category alternative happen to coincide (both pruned pairs lie within
one category), so verifying whether that equivalence survives on datasets with
a different cross-category correlation structure remains open.

\paragraph{Automated meta-validation.}
Meta-validation is currently a configured stage applied once to a fixed metric
set. A natural extension is to fold it into a fully automated loop that, given a
new dataset or model, re-derives the reliable, non-redundant metric subset and
the MQ-discounted weights without manual intervention. This would turn the
pipeline from a study-specific configuration into a reusable
``evaluate-the-evaluators'' service, and would let the framework adapt its
metric set to each new domain rather than assuming the ISIC~2017 structure
transfers.

\paragraph{More datasets and architectures.}
All evidence here comes from ISIC~2017 dermoscopy and from two convolutional
classifiers. Extending the study to a second mask-annotated medical dataset
would test whether the redundancy and reliability structure transfers across
domains, while adding a transformer-based classifier would test whether
gradient-based attributions and the layer-wise MPRT behave differently outside
the convolutional regime. Both extensions directly probe the external-validity
limits identified in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\paragraph{Additional FAE methods, including the deferred perturbation
methods.}
The evaluated panel was restricted to seven methods for reasons of
computational cost and metric compatibility; LIME and KernelSHAP were deferred
rather than dismissed. Re-introducing the perturbation-based methods---and
taming the cost of the Robustness metrics' repeated re-explanation of every
perturbed input, perhaps via caching or cheaper surrogate refits---would test
whether the framework's conclusions hold across the gradient/perturbation
divide and would let the redundancy analysis speak to whether perturbation-based
faithfulness metrics are redundant with their gradient-based counterparts.

\paragraph{Aggregation operators and weighting.}
The thesis recommends the weighted arithmetic mean but reports the minimum and
geometric-mean operators as alternatives encoding worst-dimension and
catastrophic-failure-averse stances respectively. A focused study could compare
these operators under the Autoweighted weighting (and the MQ-discount, should
it prove to matter on other data), particularly for
safety-critical interpretations where the minimum operator's
``acceptable-on-every-axis'' semantics are most defensible, and could explore
learned or preference-elicited weightings as a successor to the data-adaptive
Autoweighted scheme.

\paragraph{Regulatory alignment and auditability.}
Finally, the reproducible, meta-validated pipeline is well positioned to serve
as a transparency instrument under emerging regulation. Following
\citet{hedstrom2025bridging}, a promising direction is to connect the framework
explicitly to the transparency and documentation requirements of the EU AI Act,
formalising the pipeline's configuration record as an auditable evaluation
artefact and assessing what additional evidence---human-grounded validation in
particular---a regulator would require before a functionally grounded
effectiveness index could support a compliance claim. This would extend the
contribution from a research methodology toward a candidate evaluation standard.
